Education

Academic journey

A foundation in civil and environmental engineering, evolving into advanced research in aerosol science, IoT sensors, and machine learning.

  1. 2024 – present

    PhD, Environmental Engineering

    Indian Institute of Technology Gandhinagar

    Doctoral research
    • Flagship project: development of a patent-pending Multi-Wavelength, Multi-Angle (MWMA) optical scattering platform integrating Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) and aerosol dynamics for real-time particle size-distribution retrieval, optical characterisation (n, k, albedo), and source tracking (IN Patent App. No. 202621106781).
    • Real-time “Air Digital Twin” and time-reversal source tracking using fundamental aerosol physics (coagulation, surface deposition, room ventilation) for instantaneous contamination localization.
    • Urban sensing project: development and deployment of low-cost Environmental Quality Monitoring Systems (EQMS) for dense urban air pollution monitoring and automated field sensor calibration.
    • Deep learning architectures (GNN, LSTM/GRU, CNN-BiLSTM) for spatio-temporal PM2.5 forecasting, data fusion, and exposure modeling.
  2. 2022 – 2024

    Master of Technology (M.Tech)

    Indian Institute of Technology Ropar

    Thesis

    This study investigated indoor air pollution at IIT Ropar and evaluated indoor plants as a mitigation strategy, monitoring PM10, PM2.5, PM1, and black carbon in classrooms, hostels, and offices using real-time instruments. This first-of-its-kind systematic study at IIT Ropar integrates air quality monitoring, plant-based intervention, and health outcomes, offering a low-cost, eco-friendly solution to improve indoor air quality in educational, office, and residential settings.

    • The Ajwain plant (Trachyspermum ammi) reduced PM2.5 by 39.3%, and the Money plant (Epipremnum aureum) reduced PM10 by 38.7%.
    • Practical recommendation: 20 Ajwain or 30 Money plants per room to meet CPCB standards.
    • Spirometry revealed 35.97% with restrictive lung disease, 34.65% with obstructive patterns, and 74.59% asthma prevalence, demonstrating a strong link between indoor particulate exposure and respiratory health.
